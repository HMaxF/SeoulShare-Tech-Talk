How to Survive As Digital Nomad

⏱ Duration: 15 minutes

What Is a Digital Nomad?

The term "digital nomad" was coined by Tsugio Makimoto and David Manners in their 1997 book titled Digital Nomad. They predicted that advances in technology, such as portable devices and wireless internet, would allow people to work remotely while traveling the globe.

  1. The book: Digital Nomad discussed how technology would enable a return to a nomadic lifestyle, freeing workers from geographical constraints.
  2. The predictions: Makimoto and Manners envisioned a future where people could "live, work, and exist on the move," breaking the 10,000-year-old habit of being settled workers.
  3. The background: While the term was coined in 1997, the lifestyle gained significant traction later with the rise of widespread high-speed internet and was popularized in the 2000s by figures like Tim Ferriss.
"The digital nomad lifestyle is not just about traveling; it's about creating a life that is flexible, fulfilling, and aligned with your values."

Why Do People Choose This Lifestyle?

Many people are attracted to the freedom that comes with working remotely. They can choose where to live, explore new cultures, and design their own schedules.

Another reason is cost of living. Some digital nomads move to countries where expenses are lower, allowing them to save more money or enjoy a higher quality of life.

There is also a strong desire for personal growth. Experiencing different environments can broaden perspectives and improve adaptability.

"Living in Bali vs Seoul: It is a common dream of digital nomads to live in some exotic locations to enjoy this wonderful world than living in stressful and noisy big metropolitan cities."

What Is My Reality of Being a Digital Nomad?

My personal life experience has taught me that the reality of being a digital nomad is quite different from what it appears on social media and my dreams.

  • Longer work time: always pushing self to work longer hours due to the concern to make more income and more saving for the future.
  • Stressed and burned-out: I did and I think this is unavoidable, I realized that I have burned out when I see I've gained weight and realized that I have not exercised for a while.
  • Lonely: work and work, less time to socialize; only have time on weekends, trying harder to go out to connect to people.
  • Constantly searching: many projects should be considered as "short-term", no company will hire me for a long-term project with high pay, so always searching for the next project or opportunity to do when current project is finished.
    * Some days, I spend more time searching than doing actual current work.
  • Inconsistent sleep patterns: the irregular schedule and time zone changes can disrupt sleep cycles.
    * If you have a physical condition, such as diabetes, it can be more difficult to manage due to inconsistent routines and access to healthcare.
    * Warning: can be alcoholic.
  • Health insurance: companies may not provide the same level of coverage as traditional employment.
  • Feeling inadequate financially: the unpredictable income can lead to stress and uncertainty about the future.
    * I know that I wont die in hunger, but I still feel stressed about the financial instability.
  • As Indonesian, I face additional challenges to get visa to travel to countries, it is limiting my travel as a digital nomad.
"Being a digital nomad is about facing different set of problems than when working as a full-time employee."

What Is My Solution to Survive as a Digital Nomad?

  1. Build personal brand: Promote yourself, must have website, social media profiles.
  2. Be an extrovert: Actively build a strong network of fellow digital nomads for support and collaboration.
  3. Budgeting: Carefully manage the financial aspects of the lifestyle.
    * Especially if you have dependents or significant expenses.
    * Be expert to negotiate your pay.
    * Make sure to have a clear agreement with clients about payment terms and deadlines.
    * WARNING: You may done the work but payment may not given.
  4. Work-life balance: Maintain a healthy balance between work and personal life to avoid burnout.
  5. Health management: Exercise, get enough sun and sleep. Take care of your physical and mental health, especially when travelling.
  6. Adaptability: Freedom of work comes at a cost of constant change. Stay adaptable and open to new experiences and challenges.
  7. Partnership: Consider finding a partner who is also a digital nomad or traveling companion to share the experiences and challenges of the digital nomad lifestyle.

What Do You Need to Start? (Tools & Setup)

  • If you are travelling:
    • Reliable laptop: The most important tool is a reliable laptop that can handle your work tasks efficiently. It should be lightweight and easy to carry.
    • Internet access: Internet access is critical. Many nomads use mobile hotspots, local SIM cards, or coworking spaces to stay connected.
    • Security tools: Security tools such as VPNs are also important to protect your data when using public WiFi networks.
    • Power bank: A portable power bank is essential for keeping your devices charged while on the go.
  • If you are working remotely:
    • Comfortable workspace: Create a dedicated workspace that promotes productivity and focus in your home.
      * Neither a coworking space nor cafe.
      * Invest in furnitures.
  • Multiple communication devices: Having multiple communication devices, such as smartphones or tablets/iPads.
    • Always keep one secured and always standby at home/hotel ALL THE TIME, never bring it outside, to act as master device in case your secondary device is loss or broken.
  • Multiple email addresses.
    • One master email to reset password for all other accounts.
    • One email for work, one email for personal, one email for social media, etc. to separate the risk of being hacked or losing access to all accounts at once.
  • Backup solutions: Having reliable backup solutions is crucial to protect your work and data from loss or broken hardware.
    • Cloud storage: Use cloud-based storage services to automatically back up your files.
    • External drives: Keep physical backup drives in a safe place to ensure data safety.
    • Youtube: Store unlimited video privately.
    • Facebook: Store unlimited high-quality images.
    • Google Docs: Store documents and collaborate with others.
    • GitHub: Store code (or files) repositories.
